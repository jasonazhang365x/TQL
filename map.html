What counts. A site is here if a source says it broke ground or is under construction as of November 2026, The 33 sites from the first map are drawn with the same markers as the rest. Small edge sites under about 10 MW (roughly 20,000 sq ft) were left out.

Sources. Dark dots come from news stories or company posts about a groundbreaking or construction start. Light dots come from the under-construction lists of the Cleanview and More Than Just Parks trackers and LLM Pulse's November 15, 2026 list. Hollow dots appear only in a tracker's summary text, and some of those pages contradict themselves, so treat hollow dots as leads to check.

Not complete. The trackers count roughly 290 to 440 U.S. data center projects under construction. Their free pages show only part of the list for Texas, Virginia, Ohio, Georgia and Arizona, so smaller sites in those states are still missing.

Size is the full build-out power where a source states it. Many sites don't state a size.

Grid lines. New transmission of 230 kV and up, mostly 20 miles or longer, plus long-distance HVDC lines, from regional grid plans (ERCOT, MISO, SPP, PJM, CAISO), utility project pages and news through November 2026. Dashed lines are approved by a grid operator or regulator but not yet under construction; many still need a state route permit. Dotted lines are proposed or still in permitting. Lines finished in 2026 (SunZia, Champlain Hudson, Crossroads to Hobbs to Roadrunner) are left out. This layer replaces the 11 simplified lines from the first map.

Not drawn. Some approved lines have no public route or end substation yet, including several 765 kV lines in SPP's 2025 plan, MISO lines in Illinois, Michigan and Wisconsin, and PJM's 2026 picks in Pennsylvania, Ohio and Virginia. They are in the grid list, marked "not drawn". Utilities are also building many shorter 230 to 345 kV lines that are not here.
